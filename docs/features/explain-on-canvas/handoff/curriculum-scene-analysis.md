## Write the lesson as a story

Start with a question someone might actually ask. Give the explanation a beginning, an event that needs explaining, and a resolution. Introduce the term when it helps the reader name what just happened. Give numbers, an equation or a counterexample a role in that sequence. A teenager should be able to follow the situation, and a more experienced reader should be able to inspect the mechanism.

| Depth | What the story adds |
| --- | --- |
| Surface | One concrete situation, a plain-language definition and an ending that resolves the initial question. |
| Standard | A worked mechanism. Follow one input through the steps that produce the outcome and make the assumptions visible. |
| In Depth | A case where the first intuition fails or two explanations compete. Use a calculation, counterexample or evidence to resolve it. |

Choose a written, static, time-based or experimental presentation after the story is clear. A presentation earns its place when it makes a relationship, sequence or consequence easier to see. Depth here describes the teaching design. Product labels and available formats follow the [Tutor roadmap](./tutor-roadmap-v1.4.md#44-depth-size-and-learning-assumptions).

### AI Engineering · A library reservation that reaches the server twice

“Why did the library app tell me it failed after it already reserved the book?”

Maya asks for the library’s last copy of a graphic novel. The reservation is saved, but the reply drops on the way back. From Maya’s side, the button seems to have done nothing. She tries again.

The first request carried a label, `R17`. When the second request arrives with the same label and the same details, the server finds the saved reservation and returns it. There is still only one reservation. That is idempotency in this example. Repeating one operation leaves the same result.

If Maya later reserves a different book, that is a different operation and gets a new label. The important failure case is a crash between saving the reservation and saving its label. The design needs those records to agree after recovery, or the second request can look new again.

### Physics · The cart that stops and comes back

“How can a cart have energy when it stops against a spring?”

A cart rolls down a flat track and compresses a spring. At the deepest compression, it is motionless for an instant. Then the spring pushes it back the way it came. Its motion returned because some of its energy was stored in the stretched or compressed spring.

Suppose the spring has stiffness $k=100\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and the track has negligible friction. At $0.20\,\mathrm m$ of compression, $E=\tfrac12 kx^2=2\,\mathrm J$. At half that distance, the spring stores $0.5\,\mathrm J$. The square explains why doubling the compression stores four times as much energy. At the instant the cart stops, its kinetic energy is zero and the spring energy is at its maximum.

### Biology · The beetles left after the storm

“Can a gene variant get rarer just by chance?”

On an island, 50 beetles carry 100 copies of a marker gene between them. Half the copies are variant A. A storm hits, and only five beetles survive in this simplified example. In this model, survival is independent of the marker. Yet the survivors happen to carry only two copies of A out of ten.

Variant A fell from 50% to 20% because a small sample happened to contain fewer copies. That is genetic drift. The next generation starts with the survivors’ gene pool. If a second storm hit an identical starting population, A could rise instead. In a real population, a single before-and-after count leaves open whether selection, migration or chance caused the change.

### Chemistry · A mixture that seems to stop changing

“If the color stays the same, why do people say the reaction is still going?”

Imagine two forms of a molecule, U and V, that can change into each other in a sealed model system. At first, U becomes V faster than V becomes U, and the mixture changes. Later the color holds steady. At that point, equal numbers of molecules switch in each direction per minute.

Use rate constants of $2\,\mathrm{min}^{-1}$ for U to V and $1\,\mathrm{min}^{-1}$ for V to U. At concentrations of $3\,\mathrm{mmol/L}$ U and $6\,\mathrm{mmol/L}$ V, both rates are $6\,\mathrm{mmol/(L\,min)}$. More U raises the forward rate immediately, so the proportions shift until the rates match again. The color stopped changing because the two processes balance. The individual molecules can still switch forms.

### Mathematics · The wall that is never quite painted

“How can you keep adding pieces forever and still end up with exactly one?”

Rina paints half of a blank wall. The next day she paints half of what is left, and the next day half again. After three days she has painted $1/2+1/4+1/8=7/8$. The unpainted strip keeps getting thinner.

After $n$ days the remaining fraction is $2^{-n}$. To get it below 1%, she needs seven days, because $1/128$ is below $1/100$. Whatever positive tolerance she picks, enough halvings get the strip below it. Each finite day still leaves a strip. The limit of the total painted fraction is one.

### Economics · The school fair runs short of lanterns

“Why can lowering a price leave some people without the thing they want?”

At a school fair, buyers want more lanterns at lower prices, and sellers bring more lanterns when the price covers their effort. For this fair, suppose the quantities follow $Q_D=10-P$ and $Q_S=2+P$. At $P=4$, six lanterns are wanted and six are offered.

The organizers cap the price at $P=2$. Eight people now want a lantern, while sellers bring four. Four potential buyers miss out. The lower price helps the people who get a lantern. A queue or some other rule now decides who those people are. The model predicts a shortage of four. It needs an allocation rule to tell us who gets served.

## Review criteria

- Read the story on its own. The ending should answer the opening question, and the explanation should account for the cause of the outcome.
- Check the mechanism with one worked input and one boundary case. Preserve units, thresholds, branch meaning and any conserved quantities.
- For a real example, connect each measured claim to the actual recording, data or run. Label synthetic examples and state the assumptions that make them interpretable.
- For a generated scene, check the current card path, accessible description, labels and viewport at the intended card sizes. Use the full-depth version when testing representation limits.
