### Method

- **Local stack:** the server ran on the owner's machine (`C:/s/spec/latency-lab/explainbench/stack.sh`, wrangler dev, local D1/R2). The provider calls were real: Fireworks `accounts/fireworks/models/gpt-oss-120b` and Recraft V4.1 Flash. Settings in `.dev.vars` (`addkey-fast.cjs`): `EXPLANATION_MODEL_PROFILE=fireworks-<effort>`, `EXPLANATION_FALLBACK=off`, `EXPLANATION_INTERACTIONS=true`, `IMAGE_GENERATE_ENABLED=true`, plus the image hold and maximum-charge maps copied verbatim from `packages/web/wrangler.rabbit-hole-prod.jsonc`. `SMALL_ENV=dev` replaced the stack's `test`, so spend is enforced and every call is admitted and settled on the ledger; `USAGE_PLATFORM_DAY_USD=10` is the dev worker's value. No Anthropic key was on the stack, so no Opus call was possible. The stack was restarted for each effort with a fresh persist directory.
- **Harness** `C:/s/spec/latency-lab/explainbench/fastbench.mjs`: one test user per effort and one new canvas per card. It sends `POST /api/learn/artifact {app, command:'explanation', args, board:'main'}`, Standard Depth, timed from request to response. The server answers only after the pictures are made. Cards ran one at a time, 1.5 s apart: run 1 of all 20 prompts with the cases interleaved, then run 2. The first call after a restart is cold. Cost per card is the user's `/api/learn/usage` usedCredits before and after the card. The server log (`app.log`) gives the author call times, repair causes and picture outcomes. Prompts are in `fastprompts.mjs`.
- **Browser cards:** run 2 of the first prompt in each case, 5 per effort, sent through the canvas composer's Send (headless Chromium, `/api/learn/ask` blocked). Timed from the Send click until the text, every SVG and every picture were shown, then again after a page reload.
- Aggregation: `fastaggregate.mjs`. The blind-review set holds the 39 delivered cards: shuffled, no effort or model label, and the id-to-effort map kept separately.

### Caveats

- Factual quality is not scored here. The blind review of the 39 delivered cards is done separately by the main session.

## 4. Findings

1. No effort meets 1-4 s. The time is: one author call (low median 3.1 s), a repair on 37 of 40 low first answers (a second full call), then Recraft (median 2.2 s) after validation. The three cards that passed first time took 4.3-5.4 s.
2. Medium and high do not help: medium is slower and no more accurate (3.85 vs 4.00 factual); high does not finish inside the 8 s per-call deadline.
3. Repair causes at low: over the prose budget 20, invalid diagram 10, invalid questions or terms 4, layout 3.
4. Accuracy: 26% of delivered cards hold a factual error, mostly specific details (numbers, named objects, a reversed test).
5. Defects: a new card's picture 404s until reload (file reference not yet saved when the card first loads it); "two diagrams" is never detected as a requirement (deriveVisualRequirements matches only the singular); the asked-for visual mix is not enforced.

## 5. Visual choice study (2026-11-08, 12:49 to 13:00 PT)

Owner question: how does the author choose between an SVG TutorDiagram and a Recraft picture, and does it choose well? The example was "explain a 3D mesh", which drew a `layers` diagram (vertex / edge / face bands) where a picture of a mesh was wanted. What is scored is the **choice of visuals**: how many, picture or diagram, and each diagram's kind. The quality of a picture or an SVG is not scored.

**Method.** 42 prompts that do not name a visual type. 32 cover objects and 3D shapes, appearance, processes, comparisons, quantities, algorithms, history and abstract ideas. 10 more were added for the owner's clarification, each expecting two visuals. Three of the first 32 also expect two (heart, mitosis, virus vs bacterium: picture + diagram), so 13 prompts expect two visuals in all. The best and acceptable mix for each was written down before it was sent. Each prompt was sent once as `/explain <prompt>` to the owner's local stack (8878) as a separate test user, one at a time, Fireworks `gpt-oss-120b` low with Gemini fallback. A prompt with no card got one retry. The stack was restarted by someone else at about 12:51 PT, moving from `0703056d8` to `5fa4d674d`; that change touches maths in diagram labels only, and the visual guidance, schema and kinds are the same. Score, one criterion for all three classes: each card's visuals (how many, picture or diagram, and each diagram's kind) are matched against the mixes written before the run. Right = matches a best mix; acceptable = matches an acceptable mix; wrong = matches neither. Wrong includes an object with no picture, a picture for a data structure (pictures carry no labels) and a kind that cannot show the answer. Files: `C:/s/spec/benchmarks/explain-visual-choice-2026-11-08/` (`prompts.json`, `results.json`, `cards/` with each response, picture and rendered SVG, plus `cards/index.html`).

**Failure patterns.**
1. **Flow is the default diagram, drawn as a straight chain.** 18 of 31 diagrams are flows, most with no decision: mesh, heart, gears, bridge, water cycle, TCP handshake, mitosis, Roman Empire, merge sort, camera lens, and the four-stroke engine "cycle". The guidance pairs "a process" with diagrams, and flow comes first in the kind list with the longest description.
2. **Objects and 3D shapes get a diagram and no picture.** Mesh -> flow, castle -> comparison, gears -> flow, bridge -> flow, camera lens -> flow, Moon phases -> relationship. "Use an illustration for ... a diagram cannot draw" reads as "a diagram unless impossible". A picture is chosen only when the prompt says appearance outright: "shaped", "look like", "nebula", "dodecahedron".
3. **Two visuals are rare.** 4 of 13. Two structures to contrast always became one comparison and never two diagrams. An object plus its process got picture + diagram only 4 of 8 times.
4. **Comparison stands in for structure.** Castle parts and the atmosphere ("Atmospheric Layers") were drawn as comparison columns; the only `layers` was OSI.
5. **No charts.** Compound interest failed twice, population growth got a one-decision flow, softmax got an unlabelled picture. "Never invent numbers" leaves nothing to plot when the learner gives no numbers.
6. **Pictures for data structures.** The binary search tree and the hash table each got a picture next to a flow; the BST card has no tree.
7. **Flow is also the failure path.** Every failed send (8, plus 1 restart 503) began with an invalid flow: a decision with too few branches, two roots, a cycle, an outcome with an outgoing edge, or labels over 60 characters. The repair dropped the diagram (`explanation_missing_visual`). The Gemini fallback then answered HTTP 400 every time (9 of 9), so no card arrived.

**Recommended fixes, in priority order.** These are recommendations only; no product code was changed.
1. **Positive type rule, placed first in `EXPLANATION_GUIDANCE`.** Replace "use an illustration for a concrete scene, object or appearance a diagram cannot draw" with: "Choose by what the learner must see. For a physical object, a 3D shape, a material's structure, anatomy, a place, a living thing or what something looks like, the first visual is a picture; when the object also does something, add a diagram of that process second. For a process, sequence, relationship, comparison, hierarchy, quantity, algorithm or data structure, use diagrams. A picture has no text or labels: never use one for a data structure, an algorithm or numbers."
2. **Kind by shape, with flow restricted.** "Use flow only when the process has a decision. Otherwise use: cycle if it repeats (water cycle, engine strokes, heartbeat); sequence for messages between actors (handshakes); timeline for dates or eras; transformation when inputs become outputs (photosynthesis, softmax); tree for splits, parts or sub-calls (merge sort, recursion, a BST); relationship for causes and effects." List flow after these kinds.
3. **Per-kind cannot-draw rules.** "Layers, matrix, comparison, flow and tree cannot draw a shape, a 3D object, a place or an appearance. Comparison is for 2-4 separate items, never the parts of one thing. Layers is only for a real ordered stack (atmosphere, OSI, a call stack)."
4. **When to use two visuals.** "Use two when the question names two things to see or contrast: one each, two pictures for two appearances, two diagrams for two structures. Also use two when the question asks how an object looks and how it works."
5. **Quantity questions get an illustrative example.** "When the learner gives no numbers, choose one small example, say so in the caption and compute every plotted value exactly." Keep "never invent facts".
6. **Cheap server checks** in `explanation-visuals.js`, no LLM call:
   - (a) Refuse a flow with no decision node on the first attempt. `markdownVisuals` already reports `hasDecision`. The repair message names cycle, sequence, timeline, transformation, tree or a picture. It would have caught 11 of the 18 flows, and it costs a repair only when it fires.
   - (b) When the learner's words contain an appearance or shape cue ("look like", "shape", "appearance", "anatomy", "3D", "mesh", "solid", "lattice", "helix") and no diagram was asked for, require at least one picture, and two for "X and Y look like". This is a soft wording heuristic like the existing table cues.
7. **Separate defects found on the way.** The Gemini fallback returned HTTP 400 on all 9 fallbacks, so every Fireworks validation failure reached the learner as an error. A dropped invalid flow on the repair leaves a card with no visual instead of trying another kind.
