## ml-recurrent-training-gradient-horizons

Truncating backpropagation trades a shorter gradient path for less direct training signal from distant steps. Preserve a new small unroll, uninterrupted full backward reach, fixed cutoffs, irregular cutoffs with short and long sampled segments, and a clear difference between forward state passage and stopped backward gradients. Overview is a two-cell full/fixed reach-count heatmap, counting reachable prior states for the same final loss. Standard is a native scaled-lanes timeline: one continuous forward-state lane plus full, regular and sampled gradient-segment lanes, with intervals encoding segment length, not gradient direction. Deep is a full-width same-loss reach heatmap with all six earlier states as rows and the two short/long sampled-boundary cases as columns; ones mean a path is permitted, not a unit-valued derivative. The one Deep detail is a native connected relationship graph with states h1..h6, a final loss and a detach operation: forward h4->h5 remains present, while the backward signal takes loss->h6->h5->detach and ENDS. No gradient edge points from detach or h5 to h4. Labels distinguish forward handoff from backward signal; do not put a “stopped” label on an arrow that actually reaches the earlier state. The implicit probe changes a sampled boundary without recomputing or resetting the shared forward state. Keep topic recurrent-training and lessons empty.

Suggested keywords: `gradient segment horizons`, `forward versus detach`. useWhen: “Compare full, regular and sampled backward reach while forward state continues unchanged; not forward-state reset, numerical gradient magnitude, generic exploding gradients or a forecast horizon.”

## ml-recurrent-training-shared-parameter-gradients

Unrolling recurrence exposes all time steps whose losses contribute gradients to shared parameters. Preserve a short shared-weight unroll, per-step predictions/losses, forward state edges and a companion loss-to-prior-state grid. Overview is a simplified connected graph showing two states/losses and their shared parameter. Standard is a full native eight-node graph: h0,h1,h2,p1,p2,L1,L2 and shared a. Forward h0->h1->h2 is separate from each state's readout/prediction/loss branch; the one a node supplies both updates. Deep is a numeric sensitivity heatmap with loss rows and earlier-state columns, derived from the declared recurrence/loss; unavailable future dependency is null, not an observed zero gradient. Its detailSpec uses magnitude bars for the per-use contributions to the one shared parameter gradient, with their computed sum visible. The implicit invariant is aggregation: the same a receives the sum across its uses; it is not two independent weights, and an earlier state's gradient includes both local and later loss paths. Verify the shared derivative with an independent central finite difference. Keep topic recurrent-training and lessons empty.

Suggested keywords: `loss state dependencies`, `shared gradient uses`. useWhen: “Trace each loss through earlier recurrent states and sum contributions to one reused parameter; not independent per-step weights, a forward-only state diagram or a loss-free gradient table.”

## ml-recurrent-networks-recurrent-next-token-training

A next-token objective can teach a recurrent text model useful internal representations. Next-token training compares each recurrent output with a target shifted one position ahead. Preserve a new short observed token sequence, its prefixes, visible state per illustrated input, state handoff, predictions and observed next-token targets; mark one prediction/target pair. Overview is a prefix/state/target matrix. Standard is a native connected relationship graph, not the initially considered flow: two independent observed inputs enter their respective recurrent states, the first state supplies prior context to the second, and each state feeds a prediction compared with its own observed target. The eight nodes are two inputs, two states, two predictions and two target/loss comparisons; an explicit focus-pair label marks the first comparison. No fabricated state->observed-input or prediction->teacher-input edge is allowed. This avoids the frozen flow requirements of one root/process out-degree without inventing computation. Deep uses candidate-probability magnitude bars for a selected prefix, with computed score normalization and hidden-state loss derivative for the observed token. Its detailSpec uses two magnitude bars for a teacher-forcing state counterfactual: even if a sampled guess were wrong, the next input/prefix and its state still come from observed training tokens. Derive the hidden-state loss derivative and shared-cell derivative independently to check that the objective can train the representation; do not claim downstream usefulness is measured or guaranteed. Keep family lessons01/15, representative illustrated-bert16 lessons01/15, and d2l-rnn02 alias lessons empty.

Suggested keywords: `recurrent prefix supervision`, `teacher forced states`. useWhen: “Align observed prefixes, recurrent states and shifted next-token targets during training; not prediction-feedback rollout, transformer-only teacher forcing, downstream class labels or a measured representation-quality claim.”

## Independent toy data design

`topic-recurrent-training-data.js` supplies structural cutoff policies and the gradient example; `topic-recurrent-networks-data.js` supplies activated update/readout and token training. All counts/results derive from declared inputs/parameters, never typed display results.

- Cutoff case: six positive inputs `(1,2,1,3,2,1)`, initial state0.5, recurrence h'=0.5h+x. Boundaries: full `(0,6)`; fixed `(0,2,4,6)`; irregular A `(0,1,5,6)`; irregular B `(0,1,2,6)`. Standard's irregular-A lane contains both short and long segments. A final loss at6 may reach states1..6 under full,5..6 under fixed,6 under A,3..6 under B. A boundary detaches the state entering the next segment but never changes its forward numerical value. Compute masks from segment boundaries; do not draw a full derivative of one merely because a path exists.
- Shared-gradient case: initial h0=0.5; inputs `(1,2)`, shared a=0.5, h_t=a h_(t-1)+x_t, identity readout. Targets `(0.25,1.625)` and losses `(h_t-y_t)^2/2`. Hand check: h1=1.25,h2=2.625, both local state derivatives1. Loss/state sensitivities for columns h0,h1,h2 are `(0.5,1,null)` and `(0.25,0.5,1)`. Combined gradients for h1,h2 are1.5,1; per-use a terms0.75,1.25 sum2. Recompute from raw values and central finite differences before display rounding.
- Activated-update case: h0=0.5, inputs `(1,-2)`, a=0.5,b=1,c=0, h=ReLU(a hprev+b x+c), readout y=2h. Hand check z=(1.25,-1.375), h=(1.25,0), y=(2.5,0). These are worked scalar values, not a claim all RNNs use ReLU or this readout. The implicit zero result depends on the declared zero readout bias.
- Token-training case: observed words `birds,cross,rivers`, two training inputs `birds,cross`, targets `cross,rivers`, initial state0.5; the same toy positive-branch ReLU cell with token feature values birds1,cross2,rivers0 gives h1=1.25,h2=2.625. Vocabulary order birds,cross,rivers; readout slopes `(-1,0.5,1)` and biases `(0,1,0)`. Derive logits, stable softmax, target cross entropy, hidden sensitivities and shared-a derivative; central finite difference must agree. A hypothetical sampled `birds` at the first prefix does not replace observed `cross` in teacher-forced training. The miniature objective only demonstrates training signal into the state/parameter; no downstream quality is inferred.
