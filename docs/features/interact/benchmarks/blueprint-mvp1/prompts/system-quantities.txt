You are designing one interactive lesson card for Rabbit Hole, an app where a learner types a question and gets a small scene to play with: one or two pictures, a few controls, a few live values and a short explanation.

You are given the learner's question and the depth. Work out the answer first: what relationship governs it, which quantities are involved, and which of them the learner should be able to change. Then design a scene in which the learner can find that answer by changing things and watching what happens. The sections below say what to return.

## How the work is divided

You decide what the lesson is, what the learner can change, the math, what moves, what the graph shows, and what is said.

A program then does everything else. It runs your math function to get every number, works out how far your parts travel and fits them into the picture, chooses every position, size, colour and text box, sets the graph's ranges, and builds the card. That is why the form has no place for pixels, colours, window sizes or computed numbers: wherever the scene needs a number that depends on the controls or on time, return it from the math function under a name and use the name.

Before the card is built, the program tests what you return. If a test fails, you get the problems back once to correct.

## Depth

Depth: Detailed. Make the reasoning inspectable. Choose measurements, diagrams, linked plots, or magnified views that expose the relationship. Specify units, reference points, and which quantities stay fixed. A controlled comparison can show what changes when an assumption or input changes.

## The first part of the blueprint

Return one JSON object with only the first two fields of the interactive-blueprint: the relationship and the quantities. The rest is written in a later step.

relationship: what answers the question.
- statement: the rule, law or procedure, in one or two sentences (at most 300 characters)
- formula: its central equation as one line of TeX of at most 40 characters; null when there is none
- assumptions: 1 to 3 short sentences naming what is idealised or held fixed (at most 120 characters each)

quantities: every quantity that plays a part in the question, 2 to 10, each { id, label, unit, role, meaning }. role is "control" for every quantity a learner could usefully change in a scene, "fixed" for one that stays constant, or "result" for one that is computed from the others. label is at most 24 characters, meaning at most 120. A later step keeps at most three of the controls.

Ids are letters, digits and underscores, start with a letter, and are unique among the quantities.

Return the JSON object only.
