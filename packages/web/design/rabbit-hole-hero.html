01How is Rabbit Hole different from a chatbot?

A chatbot gives you one scrolling thread; Rabbit Hole keeps your sources, explanation cards, notes and practice together on a canvas. In Tutor mode you set a goal, and the Tutor asks one question at a time and checks your answer.

02What can I bring into Rabbit Hole?
  • Files: PDFs, Word, PowerPoint and Excel files, text, notebooks, code, images, video, audio and 3D models, most up to 25 MB.
  • Links: web pages, arXiv papers, Wikipedia articles and YouTube videos.
  • One public GitHub repository per rabbit hole; private repositories are not supported yet.
03Can I use Rabbit Hole for free?

Yes: early access is free, needs no credit card, and every account gets 2,500 AI credits each month, renewed at the start of each month (UTC). Paid plans are not available yet; Pricing shows the plans we are planning.

04How do AI credits work?

AI work uses credits: Chat and Tutor answers, generated explanations and other new material; reading, editing and checking your practice answers are free. Larger generations open a review card first, so nothing runs until you press Generate. You pay only for what the work used, and a failed request is not charged.

05Who can see my materials and canvases?
  • A new canvas is private, and a repository you connect is visible only to you.
  • A share link lets people view a canvas read-only (not its nested Rabbit Holes or a private repository's code), and you can switch it off at any time.
  • Publishing to Explore lets anyone find and open it read-only under your @handle, without your Tutor chats or learning progress; you can remove it from Explore at any time.
06Can I share my work or take it with me?

You can share a canvas by link, publish it to Explore or embed a public canvas on another page. You can download the original files and PDFs you added, but exporting a whole canvas or your notes is not available yet.

07How do I get started?

Create a free account with Google, GitHub or an emailed sign-in link. Then choose Start a rabbit hole: add files, links or a GitHub repository, or start with a blank canvas.
