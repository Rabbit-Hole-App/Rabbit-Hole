Our manifesto

Follow your
curiosity.

A question should open a path. We’re building Rabbit Hole so learning can adapt to what you know, and where you want to go.

There is a moment when a subject stops feeling distant. A question becomes personal. An explanation clicks. You want to know what comes next.

That is the moment we want to build around.

01 The beginning

Start with a real question.

A line of code you cannot explain. A paper that changes how you see a problem. An idea you keep returning to. Curiosity rarely arrives in the order of a syllabus.

You should be able to begin there. Bring the thing you want to understand, follow the first thread, and let the next question emerge from what you discover.

02 The learner

The path should adapt to you.

Two people can ask the same question and need very different explanations. One needs the missing foundation. Another needs a diagram, a worked example, or a harder problem.

Our ambition for adaptive learning is simple: begin with what you understand, notice where the explanation stops helping, and find another way in. Depth and pace should serve the person learning.

03 The work

Make understanding active.

An explanation can sound convincing before you know how to use it. Try an example. Change an assumption. Draw the relationship. Explain the idea in your own words.

We want questions, sources, interactive canvases and practice to belong to the same learning experience. Each should help you test your understanding and see what still needs attention.

04 The return

Leave a trail worth returning to.

A useful note, a source citation, an unfinished question: these are the beginnings of a map. Learning should accumulate into something you can revisit, connect and build on.

Rabbit Hole is our attempt to give that journey a home. A place to follow a question as far as it takes you, then return with a little more understanding.

There is always
another question.
