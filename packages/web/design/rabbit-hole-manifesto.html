OUR MANIFESTO

Knowledge
does not end.

Neither should learning.

For most of modern education, learning has had a shape: a beginning, a curriculum, a fixed amount of material, and an end.

That shape made sense when knowledge was expensive to distribute and a teacher's attention was scarce.

It makes less sense now.

AI changes the constraint.

THE OLD SHAPE

We taught everyone
the same way.

A university lecture might put one professor in front of a hundred students. An online course can put the same recorded lesson in front of a million.

Both are extraordinary ways to distribute knowledge. But distribution is not the same thing as adaptation.

The lesson is usually the same whether you understood the last idea instantly, missed the prerequisite three chapters ago, or already know enough to skip ahead.

The course has a length. The module has an order. The video keeps playing.

The learner adapts to the course. The course rarely adapts to the learner.

QUESTIONS

A good teacher does not
only give answers.

Socratic teaching starts somewhere more interesting than a lecture: with a question.

A good question reveals what you understand, what you assume, and where the next gap actually is.

The important part is not asking questions for the sake of asking them. It is responding to the answer.

If you already understand the intuition, move deeper. If the formula is hiding the idea, step back. If a prerequisite is missing, follow that branch first.

The next lesson should depend on what happened in the last one.

THE FINISH LINE

We made knowledge
look finite.

Finish the semester. Complete the specialization. Reach 100%. Get the certificate.

Those milestones are useful. But they can accidentally suggest that understanding has an endpoint.

In reality, every answer exposes another layer.

Learn what a Transformer does and you can ask how attention works. Understand attention and suddenly Softmax matters. Understand Softmax and you can ask about numerical stability, implementation, optimization, scaling, or the paper that changed the architecture.

There is no final module.

Knowledge is infinite.

A learning system should be able to keep going with you.

UNDERSTANDING

Knowing the words
is not enough.

Feynman's style of learning pointed at a simple test: can you make the idea clear enough to explain it?

Understanding becomes visible when you use it.

Predict what happens. Change an assumption. Work through an example. Write the code. Draw the relationship. Explain it back in your own words.

When that breaks, the failure is useful. It tells us what to teach next.

Learning should produce evidence of understanding, not just evidence of completion.

THE AI-NATIVE SHAPE

What if the path
could change?

AI gives us a chance to rethink the shape of learning itself.

Not a chatbot bolted onto a course.
Not an infinite stream of generated explanations.

What we are building: a learning environment that can notice where you are, preserve what you have already explored, and choose a better next move.

Need the intuition?
Show it.
Need the mechanism?
Interact with it.
Need the math?
Derive it.
Need the implementation?
Open the code.
Missing a prerequisite?
Go down that rabbit hole.
Ready to come back?
Return with the context intact.

The path does not have to be predetermined because the learner is no longer invisible to the system.

CONTINUITY

Learning should remember
where you left off.

Today, learning is scattered across tabs, videos, notes, papers, chats, repositories and half-finished notebooks.

You ask a great question on Tuesday and reconstruct the context from scratch on Friday.

We want learning to accumulate.

The source you opened. The diagram that finally made sense. The question you could not answer. The experiment you ran. The branch you took into a prerequisite. The explanation you came back with.

Not as a score that pretends to know how smart you are.

As a trail of evidence that helps the next learning moment start from somewhere better.

You should not have to start over every time you get curious.

WHAT WE ARE BUILDING

A home for
the next question.

Rabbit Hole is our attempt to build continuous, adaptive learning for the age of AI.

Start with the thing you actually want to understand: a repository, a paper, a concept, a problem, a question.

Learn at the depth you need.
Follow the branch that matters.
Test whether it really clicked.
Go deeper when you're ready.

And when one answer creates another question, keep going.
