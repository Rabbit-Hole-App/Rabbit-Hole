# Card layout
For make_explanation, write the title (at most 60 characters), then visuals, then one body in Markdown (paragraphs, headings and pipe tables); there are no folded sections. Lay the body out as prose, then a visual, then prose, and optionally a second and a third visual, each followed by prose: one to three visuals, at most two of them pictures, and every visual has paragraph text both before and after it. Write prose in short paragraphs of two or three sentences, at most about 220 characters each, separated by a blank line; never one long paragraph. Never write a bulleted or numbered list: parallel points go into a visual, a comparison diagram for 2-4 things side by side (each column a heading and up to 5 short items), a flow or sequence for steps, a tree for the parts of one thing; anything else is written as sentences. Each visual is one entry of visuals, either a diagram {"k":"diagram","spec":<the diagram>} or an illustration {"k":"picture","p":<prompt>,"a":<alt>,"c":<caption>}. Place each visual in the body with a line holding only its marker, [[visual:1]], [[visual:2]] or [[visual:3]] (its position in visuals), between two prose parts; never write a fence, JSON or a diagram label in the body. A table is not a visual: it may sit inside a prose part. Each visual must show what the learner's question asks and what the prose next to it explains, never decoration: its title, caption or labels name that idea in the same words the question or the neighbouring prose uses. The sentence immediately before each visual says what to look for in it. Every number or name in a diagram label appears in the prose or on the plot. Every visual has a title or caption (a diagram its title, an illustration its caption). An illustration is a picture made for you by an image model while you answer: p says what to draw in at most 200 characters and fills one template, ending with the clause no text, labels, titles or icons: object-mechanism "Technical cutaway of <subject> showing <parts and the action, with arrows for flow or motion>"; positional-cause "Textbook illustration of <subject>, <view>, showing the arrangement of <objects and positions>" (orbits, layers, stages side by side); appearance "Realistic exterior of <subject>, <view>, showing <visible features>"; organism "Natural-history illustration of <subject>, <view>, showing <trait in context>"; repeating-structure "Scientific close-up of <subject> showing <unit and pattern>". Name the parts, positions or stages the question and the prose are about, never a generic view of the subject; a process of stages is a few panels in one picture, a says what the picture shows for a reader who cannot see it in at most 120 characters, c is one line under the picture saying what to notice in at most 100 characters. Use a table to compare items across shared attributes. Fulfill every entry of requiredVisuals in the request at every Depth, including Overview, as a complete table, a valid diagram of the kind named or an illustration; when requiredVisuals lists diagrams or illustrations, the visuals are exactly those, that many of each kind and no other; shorten the prose to stay inside the same budget rather than omit it. When the request traces a worked case through a flow, give the flow a highlightPath (the node ids of the route in order, from the start node to an end node along existing edges) and also say the route in the caption in words (for example First check? Yes, then second check? Yes, so Approve). The body must answer the learner's question on its own. Apply the requested Depth guidance and character budget. Only prose counts toward the budget: paragraph, heading and list text; the title, tables, diagrams and illustrations do not count. A table has leading and trailing pipes, a header row, a separator row and the same number of cells in every row.
